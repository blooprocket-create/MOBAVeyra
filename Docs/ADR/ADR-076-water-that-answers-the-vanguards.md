# ADR-076: Water that answers the Vanguards

**Status:** Proposed. On 2026-10-12 the author asked for the river to be dynamic: "when a vanguard walks thru the water, i want the water to react." Asked whether ripples should keep to what a viewer may see, they ruled: "let the ripples show to everyone who's paying attention." This record sets how the river answers the units wading it. It becomes Accepted when the author merges the pull request that adds it.
**Date:** 2026-10-12

## Context

The river was a still surface: a flowing ripple texture and foam along its shore, the same whether a fight crossed it or not. A unit wading it left no mark, so the crossings read as painted, not as water. The author wants the water to react when a Vanguard walks through it.

The battleground's river is a generated mesh with a Single Layer Water material (ADR-073 §5), drawn on clients only; the server never draws it. Nothing in the project drew into a render target yet.

## Decision

### 1. Presentation only, on machines someone watches

`UVeyraWaterSubsystem` (VeyraUI) is a tickable world subsystem created for game worlds that are not a dedicated server's, as the other presentation subsystems are. It reads where units stand and how fast they move, and changes nothing they do: no collision, movement, sight or gameplay rule depends on the water's look.

### 2. Every unit the machine knows of stirs the water

Each unit with a unit kind, structures excepted, stirs the water while its feet are over the river (`FVeyraRiverShape::SignedDistance` below 0) and below its surface (`River.SurfaceZ`). Per the author's ruling, a unit stirs it whether or not the viewer sees the unit: the ripples are a tell for whoever is watching the river.

A unit the server never sends to this machine (Vision's fog gate, ADR-054) is not here, so it cannot stir the water here. Reaching beyond that, by sending wading units' positions to everyone, would be a new information channel through the fog. That needs its own decision and is not taken here.

### 3. Ripples simulated over a window that follows the view

The ripples are a wave height field in a pair of `RTF_RG16f` render targets (red: height now; green: height a step ago).
- **The window:** a square of water, `WindowSize` across at `Resolution` texels, centred where the camera's line of sight meets the water's plane. Its corner snaps to whole texels. When it moves, the first step reads its state shifted by the same texels, so ripples stay where they are on the water.
- **The step:** `M_CrucibleWaterStir`, generated by `ImportTerrainArt.py`, is drawn into the other target at a fixed `StepsPerSecond`, at most `MaxStepsPerFrame` a frame. Each texel takes half its neighbours' sum less its last height, kept by `Damping`, and faded toward the window's edge (`EdgeFade`) so nothing reflects off it.
- **The stirs:** each wading unit adds rings at its feet: a source of `SourceRadius`, its strength rising from `StillStrength` standing to `MovingStrength` at `FullSpeed`, pulsing at `RingsPerSecond`. The material takes `UVeyraWaterSettings::SourceSlots` (16) sources, the shader's own size. `MaxSources` of them are used, the nearest the view first.
- **The river:** M_CrucibleWater samples the latest heights through `StirHeight` and `StirWindow`. Their slope bends its surface by `StirNormalStrength`, and only their strongest crests froth (above `StirFoamFrom`, rising by `StirFoam`). Where nothing stirs it, it reads `T_Crucible_StillWater` and is as it was.

### 4. Wake and splash

While a unit wades, `NS_VeyraWaterWake` loops on the water at its feet, turned to its heading. It throws spray and spreads foam, more of both the faster the unit moves (its `Strength` from 0 standing to 1 at full speed). A unit running in at `SplashSpeed` or faster throws `NS_VeyraWaterSplash` once. Both are generated from `Effects.json` and drawn in `FoamColor`. A unit that leaves the water lets its wake settle and end.

### 5. Data, validated

Every value is in `[/Script/VeyraUI.VeyraWaterSettings]` in `DefaultGame.ini`, validated at start: the material, the river's tag, the window, step rate, damping, edge fade, sources, strengths, the parameter names, and the effects with their scales. Problems are logged and the water stays still. `ImportTerrainArt.py` reads the parameter names from the same section, so the materials and the subsystem name each parameter once. The water's look values (`stirFoam`, `frothRoughness`) live with the river's in `TerrainTextures.json`.

## Consequences

- Crossings read as water. The river shows a tell for whoever watches it, consistent with Veyra's outplay-by-attention design. The tell covers only units this machine is sent.
- **Cost:** two 512² RG16f targets and up to four full-screen-quad draws a frame, plus at most one wake system per wading unit. Measured with the packaged-performance gate of ADR-073 §6.6, still deferred.
- **Tests:** `Veyra.UI.Water.*` checks the shipped settings, refusal of broken ones, the window's whole-texel follow, the strength curve, how sources are packed, where a unit wades on the committed river, the view's focus, and that the generated ripple material takes every parameter its settings name.
- A dedicated server builds none of this. VeyraUI is client-only (ModuleLayers.json).

## Open values (provisional)

Window 6000 at 512 texels; 60 steps a second, at most 4 a frame; damping 0.985; edge fade 0.08; 16 sources of radius 60; strength 0.02 standing to 0.1 at 450 units a second; 2.5 rings a second; bend 2.0; crest foam from 0.3, rising by 1.5; splash at 150 units a second. The first in-game run, at eight times these strengths with every crest frothing, threw each unit's rings into a solid white disc; the author did not like it (2026-10-12).
