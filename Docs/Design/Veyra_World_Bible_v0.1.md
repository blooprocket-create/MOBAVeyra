# Veyra World Bible

**Version:** 0.1 — Initial Roster Era  
**Status:** Working canon. Exact geography remains intentionally provisional.

## 1. World identity

Veyra is a living world layered over the ruins of an older, continent-spanning Flux civilization. The setting must remain broad enough to support industrial frontier towns, maritime cliff cities, living elementals, memory-born Echoes, ancient machines, wild magical ecosystems, possession, Pactcraft, and ordinary biological monsters without forcing every mystery to share one origin.

### Locked principles

- Flux is foundational infrastructure and energy, but it is **not** the origin of all life or magic.
- Vanguards represent themselves first; a character is never merely a faction mascot.
- Regions can cooperate, trade, disagree internally, and share culture without becoming monolithic color-coded factions.
- The world existed before the battleground and must make sense without it.
- Mysteries are allowed to remain mysteries.

### Tone guardrails

- Do not make every civilization a reskin of the same Flux-tech culture.
- Do not make every conflict a simple nation-versus-nation war.
- Keep technology, ecology, folklore, and magic visually and culturally distinct.
- Ancient history should create consequences and questions, not become an encyclopedia dump.
- New Vanguards should reveal new parts of the world rather than merely fill gameplay archetypes.

## 2. Historical spine

### Ancient Network Era

A sophisticated civilization, or possibly several civilizations, harnessed natural Flux at continental scale. Prime Wells, Fluxways, Spires, Conductors, and artificial Fluxborn connected distant regions.

### The First Fracture

A catastrophic event broke or radically reconfigured the Flux network. Infrastructure collapsed, landscapes changed, systems corrupted, and civilizations disappeared. The exact cause is intentionally unresolved.

### The Long Rebuilding

Later cultures rebuilt unevenly. Some communities live beside and reuse ancient infrastructure; some avoid it; some developed traditions that barely use Flux at all.

### The Present Resurgence

Dormant systems are activating again. Wells erupt, corrupted nodes awaken, old routes reopen, and modern powers compete over the returning network.

### Intentionally unanswered

- Who built the oldest layers of the network.
- What directly caused the First Fracture.
- Whether the current resurgence is natural, cyclical, deliberate, or a warning.
- How much Relay truly remembers.
- What Torr's Fluxborn instincts reveal about the old network.

## 3. Forces, life, and the unexplained

| Phenomenon | Working canon |
|---|---|
| **Flux** | Natural energy/network phenomenon harnessed through ancient and modern engineering. |
| **Fluxborn** | Temporary constructs generated through the network. Torr is a unique persistent self-aware exception. |
| **Echoes** | Memory made self-sustaining through repetition. Tavi and Vera are not ghosts. |
| **Pactcraft** | A Drowned Cantons tradition of entering agreements with beings from the Elsewhere. |
| **The Elsewhere** | An adjacent existence, not normal geography on Veyra. Nix originates there. |
| **Wakebound** | Rare people returned by the sea changed. Neris is a known Wakebound. |
| **Forgehearts** | Elemental consciousnesses formed through repeated heat, pressure, alloying, and transformation. Varkesh is one. |
| **Wildlight** | Old natural magic running through untouched ecosystems. Moro channels it instinctively. |
| **Living landscape** | Silt demonstrates that earth, water, sediment, remains, memory, and time can become conscious without being Fluxborn. |
| **Spirits / possession** | Patch proves independent spiritual possession exists; the entity inside him remains mysterious. |
| **Native fauna** | Veyra has biological lineages capable of becoming monstrous without Flux or magic. Korruk is an animal. |

## 4. The Iron March

A huge industrial frontier of freight settlements, old highways, tunnels, canyons, bridges, salvage yards, and refineries built around surviving transportation infrastructure.

Practical skill carries enormous social value. Vehicles are survival equipment. Repair, route knowledge, transport, salvage, and local autonomy matter more than formal prestige.

The March uses Flux technology aggressively but pragmatically. Competition over ancient transport infrastructure makes it strategically important.

**Known Vanguards:** Raska and Kade.  
**Known settlement:** Calder's Run.

### Relationships

- The Iron March and Ember Basin are established allies.
- The March depends on the Basin for forged components and advanced metallurgy.
- The Basin benefits from March transport, repair, and integration.
- External attempts to seize routes or infrastructure create recurring conflict.

### Open questions

- Exact political structure of the March.
- Which outside powers currently pressure it.

## 5. The Ember Basin

A volcanic/geothermal foundry region of black mountains, furnaces, crucibles, metalworks, and cities shaped by heat and fabrication.

If the Iron March keeps things moving, the Ember Basin makes the things worth moving.

Forgehearts arise here. They are not transformed humans, constructs, or Fluxborn; they are true elemental consciousnesses born from molten alloy under repeated heat, pressure, smelting, and forging.

**Known Vanguard:** Varkesh, the Forgeheart.

### Relationships

- Longstanding industrial alliance with the Iron March.
- Trade, engineering, and shared infrastructure bind the regions beyond a simple political treaty.
- Varkesh has worked with March engineers for decades.

## 6. The Drowned Cantons

Basalt island chains, cliff cities, rope bridges, hanging markets, huge harbors, and violent seas. Stable Flux Wells are scarce, helping drive the development of Pactcraft and other maritime traditions.

The Cantons are cosmopolitan ports where an outsider can become local through participation rather than bloodline.

**Known Vanguards:** Marek + Nix, Neris, Qazharr.

### Cultural anchors

- Pactcraft is agreement and obligation, not conventional demon summoning.
- Rescue culture and seafaring shape the region.
- Qazharr was born on a distant coast but considers the Cantons home.
- His original homeland remains deliberately unnamed until the world needs it.

## 7. Merrin

Merrin is a ruined city where repeated human experiences became embedded strongly enough to create Echoes.

Echoes are not ghosts of specific dead people. They are personhood formed from repeated memory.

**Tavi** is Merrin remembering how it lived: play, games, chasing, hiding, childhood patterns.

**Vera** is Merrin remembering how it died: firing lines, siege commands, volleys, repetition, war.

There may be more Echoes. Merrin should remain a source of strange memory-born life without turning every Echo into the same personality or power set.

## 8. The Shatterdeep

A harsh region of uplifted stone shelves, jagged mineral canyons, caves, subterranean water, mineral-rich fungi, and extreme native ecology.

**Known Vanguard:** Korruk, the Splinterbeast.

Korruk is biological fauna. His species evolved mineralized bone armor, hollow spines, and pressure organs naturally. He is not an Echo, Flux mutation, construct, or disguised person.

Industrial demand for the species' mineralized spines brought hunters and miners into breeding grounds. Korruk follows the resonance of harvested material and destroys stockpiles containing it.

## 9. The Wildwood / Verdant Depths

An immense old-growth wilderness where living canopy, rivers, cliffs, ruins, and untouched ecosystems overwhelm the marks of civilization.

**Known Vanguard:** Moro, the Wildspark.

Wildlight runs through the region as natural magic distinct from Flux technology. Moro channels it instinctively.

Human settlement is secondary to the land itself. The region can support future nature-linked and non-humanoid Vanguards without making them members of one unified faction.

## 10. The Buried Riverlands

**Working name.**

An ancient buried system of riverbeds, clay, sand, ruins, collapsed settlements, forgotten graves, and underground watercourses layered beneath newer lands.

**Known Vanguard:** Silt.

Silt is not Fluxborn. His consciousness arose from accumulated sediment, water, remains, repeated history, and memory carried through the buried river system.

The region should make archaeology and geology supernatural without reducing them to standard necromancy.

### Open questions

- Final regional name.
- Surface cultures and settlements.
- Whether Silt is unique or one of several landscape consciousnesses.

## 11. The ancient Flux network

Prime Wells, Fluxways, Spires, Fluxborn systems, and Conductors belong to an older infrastructure layer.

**Relay** is one of the last known Conductors: an ancient mobile power/maintenance machine built for network construction rather than war.

Ancient Fluxborn were used as workers as well as battlefield constructs.

Modern societies use fragments of a system they do not fully understand.

The Meridian Crucible is one surviving paired-Prime-Well installation from this era.

## 12. Political texture

Politics should be messy rather than faction-color-coded.

- **Iron March + Ember Basin:** established allied regions with old industrial ties.
- **Drowned Cantons:** strong maritime local identity and broad trade; not currently locked into a larger alliance bloc.
- **Merrin:** a ruined city, not a functioning state.
- **Wildwood:** geographic/ecological region, not one political actor.
- **Shatterdeep:** contested frontier ecology; political claims remain open.
- **Vanguards:** represent personal histories and loyalties. Allies can disagree; enemies can be friends.

## 13. Battleground canon boundary

The Meridian Crucible is a real place.

Individual matches are **not literal historical canon**.

Resonant Forms allow Vanguards who are lovers, allies, relatives, or enemies to appear on either side of a match without rewriting their real relationships.

Character lore tells who these people are. A match shows what could happen if the Crucible placed those Vanguards on opposite sides.

## 15. Worldbuilding priorities

- Name and place modern nations only when needed; do not force every region to equal one nation.
- Reveal the lost civilization gradually through ruins, machines, artifacts, and contradictory records.
- Keep the First Fracture's exact cause unresolved until the larger story benefits from an answer.
- Name Qazharr's distant homeland when a future story actually reaches it.
- Keep a meaningful portion of the wider world intentionally unknown during early development.
