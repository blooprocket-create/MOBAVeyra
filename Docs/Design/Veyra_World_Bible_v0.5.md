# Veyra World Bible

**Version:** 0.5 — 25-Vanguard current design roster  
**Status:** Working canon; geography remains provisional. All 25 designed Vanguards belong to the current roster target, which can grow further; their design does not imply release or implementation.

## 1. World identity

Veyra is a living world layered over the ruins of an older, continent-spanning Flux civilization. The setting must remain broad enough to support industrial frontier towns, maritime cliff cities, living elementals, memory-born Echoes, ancient machines, wild magical ecosystems, possession, Pactcraft, and ordinary biological monsters without forcing every mystery to share one origin.

### Locked principles

- Flux is foundational infrastructure and energy, but it is **not** the origin of all life or magic.
- Vanguards represent themselves first; a character is never merely a faction mascot.
- Regions can cooperate, trade, disagree internally, and share culture without becoming monolithic color-coded factions.
- The world existed before the battleground and must make sense without it.
- Mysteries are allowed to remain mysteries.

### Tone guardrails

- Do not make every civilization a reskin of the same Flux-tech culture.
- Do not make every conflict a simple nation-versus-nation war.
- Keep technology, ecology, folklore, and magic visually and culturally distinct.
- Ancient history should create consequences and questions, not become an encyclopedia dump.
- New Vanguards should reveal new parts of the world rather than merely fill gameplay archetypes.

## 2. Historical spine

### Ancient Network Era

A sophisticated civilization, or possibly several civilizations, harnessed natural Flux at continental scale. Prime Wells, Fluxways, Spires, Conductors, and artificial Fluxborn connected distant regions.

### The First Fracture

A catastrophic event broke or radically reconfigured the Flux network. Infrastructure collapsed, landscapes changed, systems corrupted, and civilizations disappeared. The exact cause is intentionally unresolved.

### The Long Rebuilding

Later cultures rebuilt unevenly. Some communities live beside and reuse ancient infrastructure; some avoid it; some developed traditions that barely use Flux at all.

### The Present Resurgence

Dormant systems are activating again. Wells erupt, corrupted nodes awaken, old routes reopen, and modern powers compete over the returning network.

### Intentionally unanswered

- Who built the oldest layers of the network.
- What directly caused the First Fracture.
- Whether the current resurgence is natural, cyclical, deliberate, or a warning.
- How much Relay truly remembers.
- What Torr's Fluxborn instincts reveal about the old network.

## 4. The Iron March

A huge industrial frontier of freight settlements, old highways, tunnels, canyons, bridges, salvage yards, and refineries built around surviving transportation infrastructure.

Practical skill carries enormous social value. Vehicles are survival equipment. Repair, route knowledge, transport, salvage, and local autonomy matter more than formal prestige.

The March uses Flux technology aggressively but pragmatically. Competition over ancient transport infrastructure makes it strategically important.

**Known Vanguards:** Raska, Kade, Mavra, and Eudora Blackbridge.  
**Known settlement:** Calder's Run (Raska, Kade, and Mavra; Eudora's specific hometown is not established).

### Calder's Run

Calder's Run is an industrial March settlement shaped by garages, freight work, salvage, refinery infrastructure, practical engineering, and a strong culture of solving dangerous problems locally.

Raska, Kade, and Mavra establish different parts of the settlement's identity:

- **Raska** reflects its mechanics, couriers, improvisers, risk-takers, and local folk-hero culture.
- **Kade** reflects its engineering, surveying, route knowledge, and precision work.
- **Mavra** reflects its refinery crews, containment specialists, industrial chemistry, material sorcery, and workplace culture.

Eudora Blackbridge represents the wider Iron March's freight-route maintenance and work-crew safety culture. Her single modular Picket platform began as portable cover for bridge crews and later gained a rivet cannon. She knows Raska, Kade, and Mavra through engineering work, but **is not established as a Calder's Run native**.

Mavra's profession, the **spillwright**, is a Calder's Run / Iron March practical discipline combining containment work, industrial chemistry, and magic used to manage hazardous reactions.

The **South Sump Failure** is a known Calder's Run industrial disaster. Mavra became locally prominent after containing the cascading chemical failure and exposing deferred maintenance that contributed to the event.

### Relationships

- The Iron March and Ember Basin are established allies.
- The March depends on the Basin for forged components and advanced metallurgy.
- The Basin benefits from March transport, repair, and integration.
- External attempts to seize routes or infrastructure create recurring conflict.

### Open questions

- Exact political structure of the March.
- Which outside powers currently pressure it.

## 5. The Ember Basin

A volcanic/geothermal foundry region of black mountains, furnaces, crucibles, metalworks, and cities shaped by heat and fabrication.

If the Iron March keeps things moving, the Ember Basin makes the things worth moving.

Forgehearts arise here. They are not transformed humans, constructs, or Fluxborn; they are true elemental consciousnesses born from molten alloy under repeated heat, pressure, smelting, and forging.

**Known Vanguards:** Varkesh, the Forgeheart, and Gorraveth, the Slagmaw.

The Basin also has biological reptilian inhabitants distinct from its molten-metal Forgehearts. Gorraveth is a scarred reptilian hunter and mine-retrieval survivor, carrying burned work armor and viscous industrial slag after surviving an unsafe excavation collapse. Renewed old-network disturbances threaten the Basin's deep tunnels and displace wildlife. “Drakari” is a **provisional visual-sheet lineage name**, not a settled classification of all Basin peoples. Varkesh knows Gorraveth by reputation but finds his manners exhausting.

### Relationships

- Longstanding industrial alliance with the Iron March.
- Trade, engineering, and shared infrastructure bind the regions beyond a simple political treaty.
- Varkesh has worked with March engineers for decades.

## 6. The Drowned Cantons

Basalt island chains, cliff cities, rope bridges, hanging markets, huge harbors, and violent seas. Stable Flux Wells are scarce, helping drive the development of Pactcraft and other maritime traditions.

The Cantons are cosmopolitan ports where an outsider can become local through participation rather than bloodline.

**Known Vanguards:** Marek + Nix, Neris, Qazharr, Sylra, Tavra.

### Cultural anchors

- Pactcraft is agreement and obligation, not conventional demon summoning.
- Rescue culture and seafaring shape the region.
- Qazharr was born on a distant coast but considers the Cantons home. He left piracy to settle there; Tavra, his former first mate and master gunner, was the only crewmate who stayed. She rebuilt her heavy Flux cannon Mournwake to protect the harbor. Neither character's original distant homeland is named.
- His original homeland remains deliberately unnamed until the world needs it.
- **Bellcraft / fog-guidance traditions** are practiced by harbor pilots, rescuers, and chartkeepers. They use tuned bells, sea-glass, resonance, moisture, waymarks, and controlled mist to navigate conditions where direct sight is unreliable.
- Bellcraft is distinct from Pactcraft, Wakebound magic, and Flux engineering.
- Sylra represents this harbor-pilot tradition and its philosophy that presence, direction, and safe movement can matter even when exact visual information is unavailable.

## 7. Merrin

Merrin is a ruined city where repeated human experiences became embedded strongly enough to create Echoes.

Echoes are not ghosts of specific dead people. They are personhood formed from repeated memory.

**Tavi** is Merrin remembering how it lived: play, games, chasing, hiding, childhood patterns.

**Vera** is Merrin remembering how it died: firing lines, siege commands, volleys, repetition, war.

**Vitra** is Merrin remembering the light of homecoming: shattered stained-glass windows and the repeated memory of ordinary people waiting for someone to return gave rise to a glass-bodied Echo. She is not a ghost of a named person, not a Fluxborn construct, and is distinct in origin and outlook from Tavi's play and Vera's war.

Merrin should remain a source of strange memory-born life without turning every Echo into the same personality or power set.

## 8. The Shatterdeep

A harsh region of uplifted stone shelves, jagged mineral canyons, caves, subterranean water, mineral-rich fungi, and extreme native ecology.

**Known Vanguards:** Korruk, the Splinterbeast, and Aurelisse, the Open Sky.

The upper Shatterdeep includes inhabited cliff settlements linked by narrow bridges, dangerous exposed paths, and resonant mineral canyons. Chimes and route-guidance traditions help travelers navigate fierce winds. **Aurelisse** is a naturally formed wind-and-mineral-dust elemental who learned to guide people through storms; newly disturbed ancient Flux infrastructure is making crossings and weather less predictable. Her emergence is not a Merrin-style memory Echo or a consequence of Bramblehollow's experimentation.

Korruk is biological fauna. His species evolved mineralized bone armor, hollow spines, and pressure organs naturally. He is not an Echo, Flux mutation, construct, or disguised person.

Industrial demand for the species' mineralized spines brought hunters and miners into breeding grounds. Korruk follows the resonance of harvested material and destroys stockpiles containing it.

## 9. The Wildwood / Verdant Depths

An immense old-growth wilderness where living canopy, rivers, cliffs, ruins, and untouched ecosystems overwhelm the marks of civilization.

**Known Vanguard:** Moro, the Wildspark.

Wildlight runs through the region as natural magic distinct from Flux technology. Moro channels it instinctively.

Human settlement is secondary to the land itself. The region can support future nature-linked and non-humanoid Vanguards without making them members of one unified faction.

## 10. The Buried Riverlands

**Working name.**

An ancient buried system of riverbeds, clay, sand, ruins, collapsed settlements, forgotten graves, and underground watercourses layered beneath newer lands.

**Known Vanguards:** Silt and Cairn.

Silt is not Fluxborn. His consciousness arose from accumulated sediment, water, remains, repeated history, and memory carried through the buried river system.

Cairn arose independently from compressed riverstone, old bridge foundations, collapsed masonry, and buried iron within the same riverlands. He is an asymmetrical, towering guardian whose hooked arm and immovable stone body symbolize what the river buried and left behind. Renewed Flux-network disturbances awakened him but did not create him. Cairn and Silt share a region, not a familial bond or a universal explanation of elemental consciousness.

The region should make archaeology and geology supernatural without reducing them to standard necromancy.

### Open questions

- Final regional name.
- Surface cultures and settlements.
- What other landscape consciousnesses, if any, formed beyond the known Silt and Cairn.


## 11. The Reed Provinces

**Working regional name.**

A mountainous region of old valleys, dense bamboo and reed forests, fortress towns, terraced settlements, shrines, narrow passes, and ancient roads.

Two powerful martial institutions shape much of the region's political and cultural order.

### The Blade House

**Working name.**

The public-facing martial power.

Its tradition emphasizes:

- formal swordsmanship;
- dueling;
- military discipline;
- sworn territory and obligations;
- retainers, officers, and visible authority.

The Blade House's concept of duty can be extremely strict about **where** and **to whom** its obligations apply.

### The Veil House

**Working name.**

The covert martial power.

Its tradition emphasizes:

- infiltration;
- intelligence networks;
- assassination;
- misdirection;
- concealment and shadow techniques;
- indirect political influence.

The Veil House often prioritizes larger covert objectives over immediate visible intervention.

### Relationship between the houses

The houses are rivals, but their competition also helps define the region's existing political order.

They should not be reduced to "good samurai versus evil ninjas" or the reverse. Both contain honorable people, compromised people, practical people, and institutional failures.

**Known Vanguard:** Angeru, the Housebreaker.

### Angeru and the village

Angeru came from a small village caught between the houses' spheres of influence.

A violent criminal gang repeatedly extorted settlements in the area. The village appealed to both houses for aid.

The Blade House refused because the village lay outside its formally sworn territory and intervention risked escalation with the Veil House.

The Veil House knew of the gang but withheld intervention because acting would expose agents involved in a larger operation.

The gang later destroyed the village and killed Angeru's family.

Angeru's grievance is therefore not simply that the houses were weak or malicious. It is that **both were powerful enough to help and possessed institutional logic that made non-intervention acceptable**.

He later learned both houses' martial traditions and now uses those traditions against the institutions themselves.

His goal is to dismantle their leadership, political monopoly, and control over martial knowledge — not automatically to kill every person carrying either crest.

### Regional design guardrails

- Do not make the Blade House a generic "honor faction."
- Do not make the Veil House a generic "evil assassin faction."
- Their rivalry should have real political, territorial, and historical consequences.
- Ordinary villages and local communities must exist independently of the two houses.
- Angeru's critique of the houses is emotionally central, but future characters from either house should be able to present credible opposing perspectives.


## 12. Bramblehollow

A hidden wooded valley of immense flowering trees, winding streams, overgrown old Flux machinery, suspended bridges, miniature lifts, and connected settlements built at dramatically different physical scales. Its residents are collectively called **Bramblekin**: several distinct animal-humanoid peoples with foxlike, harelike, rodent, otterlike, and other lineages. They are adults with their own institutions, professions, families, and opinions—not comic-relief mascots, transformed humans, or one uniform faction. Species appearance does not universally determine personality or magical ability.

### Earliest Flux experimentation

During the **earliest days of Flux discovery**, before the mature continent-spanning network, multiple ancient research programs experimented on living organisms using concentrated Flux, producing self-aware animal-humanoid subjects. The surviving records are incomplete; exact methods, motives, researchers, and whether subjects consented are deliberately unresolved, not a single known research agenda. Survivors became stable, biologically reproducing lineages and eventually built autonomous communities after the old facilities were abandoned. Their descendants are **biological people, not artificial summoned Fluxborn**. Neither Aurelisse's natural elemental origin nor unrelated native fauna and Merrin Echoes should be retroactively explained by these experiments.

### Thistlewick

An active village-town clustered around colossal tree roots and branches, with markets, workshops, walkways, lifts, tunnels, and couriers linking residents of very different sizes. **Mimzi** grew up in a family of small Flux-device repairers here; **Celandrine** carries messages along the difficult routes to other settlements. Bramblehollow's sense of self-determination can be summarized as: “We were someone's experiment. What we became is ours.” Individuals may disagree over their heritage and the fate of ancient machinery.

### The Root Vaults

Abandoned, overgrown research laboratories beneath Bramblehollow, with some systems still partially active. Fragmentary records name Bramblekin ancestors as experimental subjects but do not provide a comprehensive origin story. Modern residents have differing attitudes toward preserving, studying, restricting, or dismantling the devices. Mimzi's excavations and attempts at salvage have exposed their dangers; renewed disturbances have damaged Celandrine's familiar courier routes.

**Known Vanguards:** Mimzi, the Pocket Hex, and Celandrine, the Wayrunner.

## 13. The ancient Flux network

Prime Wells, Fluxways, Spires, Fluxborn systems, and Conductors belong to an older infrastructure layer.

**Relay** is one of the last known Conductors: an ancient mobile power/maintenance machine built for network construction rather than war.

Ancient Fluxborn were used as workers as well as battlefield constructs.

Modern societies use fragments of a system they do not fully understand.

The Meridian Crucible is one surviving paired-Prime-Well installation from this era.

## 14. Political texture

Politics should be messy rather than faction-color-coded.

- **Iron March + Ember Basin:** established allied regions with old industrial ties.
- **Drowned Cantons:** strong maritime local identity and broad trade; not currently locked into a larger alliance bloc.
- **Merrin:** a ruined city, not a functioning state.
- **Wildwood:** geographic/ecological region, not one political actor.
- **Shatterdeep:** contested frontier ecology; political claims remain open.
- **Reed Provinces:** region shaped by rival martial houses whose influence extends into law, protection, intelligence, and local politics. Exact political structure remains open.
- **Bramblehollow:** autonomous biological communities with varied attitudes toward inherited experimental history, not a functioning ancient research state or a unified ideological faction.
- **Vanguards:** represent personal histories and loyalties. Allies can disagree; enemies can be friends.

## 15. Battleground canon boundary

The Meridian Crucible is a real place.

Individual matches are **not literal historical canon**.

Resonant Forms allow Vanguards who are lovers, allies, relatives, or enemies to appear on either side of a match without rewriting their real relationships.

Character lore tells who these people are. A match shows what could happen if the Crucible placed those Vanguards on opposite sides.

## 17. Worldbuilding priorities

- Name and place modern nations only when needed; do not force every region to equal one nation.
- Reveal the lost civilization gradually through ruins, machines, artifacts, and contradictory records.
- Keep the First Fracture's exact cause unresolved until the larger story benefits from an answer.
- Name Qazharr's distant homeland when a future story actually reaches it.
- Finalize the Reed Provinces, Blade House, and Veil House names only when their broader culture and politics are developed; all three are currently working names.
- Keep Bramblehollow's original research programs, motives, consent history and complete methods unresolved until a future story needs the answers; contemporary Bramblekin have independent lives beyond their origins.
- Keep Eudora's hometown and Gorraveth's wider lineage classification open until needed.
- Keep a meaningful portion of the wider world intentionally unknown during early development.
